Mini splits exist for the rooms central air forgets. The garage workshop, the sunroom that turns into an oven by midafternoon, the addition the ducts never reached, the Murray house that was built without ductwork at all: each of these can be heated and cooled on its own by one or more wall-mounted heads. A slim line set carries refrigerant, power, and condensate drainage through a single three-inch wall penetration to the outdoor unit, so there is no duct system to leak. The inverter compressor speeds up and slows down with the load rather than cycling hard, which keeps the room steady, quiet, and cheap to run. In winter, the same equipment works as a cold-climate heat pump, not a cooling unit pretending to heat.

Many disappointing mini splits were doomed on paper, not on the wall. The classic error is oversizing: a head too big for its room cools in a hurry, shuts off before it pulls out the humidity, and never settles into a steady rhythm. Murray HVAC sizes every zone to its real load instead. On multi-zone systems we also check that the outdoor unit's capacity is balanced across the heads it feeds and that every line set stays within the manufacturer's length and lift limits. Then comes placement. We walk the Murray home with you, weigh wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor-console options, and put each head where it moves air well and still looks right, with outdoor line sets dressed in line-hide rather than left bare.

On installation day, the small steps that customers never see are the ones we never rush. Every flare is formed carefully, torqued to spec, and leak-checked, because a leaking flare joint is the number one way mini splits fail. The line set gets a nitrogen pressure test, then a deep vacuum confirmed on a micron gauge, before refrigerant ever moves. We make sure condensate drains with proper fall or a dependable pump, trim the charge for the actual run length, and test each zone in heating and cooling. The Benefits of Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation

Since a mini split looks like a simple box on a wall, a lot of them get installed like one, and they fail in predictable ways. The weakest link is the flare joint. Form one badly or leave it under-torqued and it seeps refrigerant slowly, so nobody notices until the head is blowing room-temperature air months later. Skip the nitrogen test and the measured vacuum, and moisture stays in a circuit that inverter compressors really dislike. Push the line set past the manufacturer's length or lift limits and you lose capacity without a single alarm. Forget the drain slope and the first muggy week leaves water marks on the drywall.

What separates good ductless work from bad is discipline: sizing. Even the smallest indoor heads are oversized for plenty of bedrooms, and an oversized head short-cycles, leaves moisture in the air, and never finds a steady state. Murray HVAC sizes zones honestly and installs them with the same care, torquing and testing every flare, proving the line set with nitrogen, pulling a measured vacuum, adjusting charge for the run, and commissioning each head in both modes. The refrigerant side is federally regulated and the electrical side calls for a properly sized circuit and disconnect, so neither is a weekend project. What you get is a system that stays silent and efficient for fifteen years instead of a rushed install that never quite works.

Ductless Mini Split Installation Prevention Tips

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Schedule professional cleaning of the indoor coil and blower wheel before buildup turns into growth
  • Resist turning the system off and on; an inverter is most efficient holding one steady temperature
  • Schedule service once a year so charge, flares, and drainage get verified before small issues grow
  • Clean the washable filter screens in each head about once a month, since they clog faster than furnace filters
  • Check the condensate outlet during cooling season to make sure water is actually coming out
  • Brief defrost cycles and a little steam in winter are normal, but a unit encased in ice needs service
  • Clear snow, leaves, and clippings away from the outdoor unit year-round
