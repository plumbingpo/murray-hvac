When Your System Fails in St. George

When your system quits, how quickly help arrives matters more than anything else. For that reason, we keep scheduling flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Emergency calls usually get a technician within a day, and we treat no-heat and no-cool calls ahead of routine work.

The emergency calls we answer usually follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's an AC blowing warm air, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil iced over. On the heating side, it's a furnace that lights and then shuts down. And any time of year, it's breakers that trip every cycle, plugged condensate drains backing water into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that have quit. Since our trucks carry capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, most of these are wrapped up in one visit, with the price agreed before any work begins.

Two common situations call for a quick step from you before a technician arrives. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: switch the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can flood the drain pan and hurt the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or gives off a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and call us. That small step can prevent a far bigger repair.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in St. George

A little routine care makes all the difference between a system that lasts and one that quits in the middle of a heat wave. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, at least two feet of open space around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and two professional tune-ups a year, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

How old is your system? Its age says a lot about what comes next. Most well-maintained air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is past that range and is also showing higher bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or repeated repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install when the first heat wave hits.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor ignored for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and miserable nights.
